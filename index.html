Self-Drive, Start To Finish

What a bareboat rental actually means.

Step One

You bring the license

Because you're the one driving, Canadian law requires proof of boating competency — a Pleasure Craft Operator Card (PCOC) or a recognized equivalent. No PCOC, no shame: book a skippered trip instead and a licensed captain drives.

Step Two

We prep the boat

The RIB is fuelled, life jackets are aboard for everyone in your group, and all Transport Canada–required safety and navigation gear is packed before you get anywhere near the dock.

Step Three

You get a local briefing

A quick walkthrough on the dock — current conditions, fuel range, and where locals actually go — then the boat is yours until your time's up.

Where To Point The Bow

Your route, your call.

That's the whole appeal of driving yourself — nobody else's itinerary. A few favorites other renters come back with stories about:

Britannia Beach

The easiest first leg south of Squamish — usually calm water, and a good stretch to get comfortable at the helm before pushing further out.

Porteau Cove

Marine park and popular dive site partway down the Sound. Cut the engine here and just sit with the view for a while.

Anvil Island

The landmark you can see from town. Quiet water around its bays, and a solid chance of seals or eagles if you go slow.

The coves with no road

Howe Sound has beaches and inlets a car simply can't reach. With the boat in your hands, that's not a problem — it's the point.

Before You Book

Self-drive rental FAQ

Do I need a license to drive it myself?

Yes. Because you're behind the wheel, Canadian law requires proof of boating competency — most commonly a Pleasure Craft Operator Card (PCOC) or a recognized equivalent from your home country. Don't have one? Ask about a skippered trip instead, where a licensed captain drives for you.

What's included with the rental?

The 18-ft RIB and its 50 HP outboard, life jackets for your whole group, all required navigation and safety equipment, and a dock-side walkthrough before you cast off.

How is this different from a boat tour?

On a tour, a licensed captain drives and you ride along for a set route. Here, the boat is yours for the time you book — you set the course.

How much does it cost?
